05

The competitive landscape

The "LLM cost" space looks crowded, but nearly everyone is doing the same thing: showing you what you spent. The audit-and-recover motion is structurally empty.

The whole category surfaces spend; we recover it. Observability tools (Langfuse, Helicone, LangSmith, Braintrust) and gateways (Bifrost, LiteLLM, Portkey) give you per-request token attribution, dashboards, and budget alerts. They tell you what you spent — never what you should have been billed. The refund-automation tools (Fini, Quadrant) handle your customers' refunds, not your own provider bill. No one independently audits the provider's invoice for correctness and recovers the difference. That gap is the entire product.

05·A

What makes us stand out

Five structural advantages — each one a thing the monitoring tools cannot do without becoming a different product.

1 Audit, not observe

Dashboards show what you spent. We hold a versioned rate-card history and compute what you should have been billed — then recover the gap. Different verb, different buyer (the CFO acts on an audit, not a dashboard).

2 Enforcement, not just alerts

The proxy is the only place waste is prevented live — dedup collapsed, over-tiered calls downgraded, caps enforced. Monitoring tools alert after the spend; we stop it.

3 Provider-independent

We audit spend on providers, not for them — so we're trusted the way a bank's own statement never is. The Anthropic billing-bug episode (overcharge, refund denied) is exactly why independent audit exists.

4 Success-fee, not another SaaS seat

Monitoring tools add cost on top of your AI bill. Our enterprise tier is no recovery, no charge — aligned to dollars returned, not seats sold. Easiest possible procurement yes.

5 Moat compounds with complexity

Every new token type, caching mode, and reasoning tier adds a billing axis customers didn't account for. The audit's value grows with the pricing surface, not against it.

6 Partner, not collide

Observability tools become distribution, not competition — their telemetry is the input layer our audit sits on top of. We meet customers who already have Helicone or Langfuse.

R3

Ongoing bets — what we build in parallel

The milestones are discrete features. These are continuous investments that run across all of them — the work that converts a feature ladder into a defensible position. None of them "ship and stop"; they compound.

TEL

Our own telemetry layer

Continuous · foundational

Today we read provider usage APIs and local journals. Over time we build first-party instrumentation — our own SDK-level capture of every request, token type, and response — so we're not dependent on what a provider chooses to expose.

Why it compounds: owning the measurement layer means we see signals providers don't surface (per-request billing match, error-billed calls, latency) and aren't blinded when a provider changes its API.
AGT

Autonomous audit & refund agents

Continuous · automation

Recovery starts as drafted claim packets. The trajectory is autonomous agents running in loops (the Ralph-loop pattern — fresh-context iterations until the goal is met) that detect a leak, assemble the evidence, file the provider claim, and follow it to credit — with human review only above a threshold.

Why it compounds: the same autonomous-loop technique we guard against for runaway customer spend is what powers our own audit agents — we both enforce loop safety and run loops safely. Every recovered case trains the agent on what wins.
RC

Rate-card & leak intelligence

Continuous · the moat

A versioned history of every provider's pricing and a cross-account library of leak patterns. Every audit we run makes this sharper — benchmark bands, the 80/20 leak split, what "normal" looks like per workload.

Why it's the moat: a competitor can copy a feature in a sprint; they cannot copy years of pricing history and cross-account leak data. This is the asset that gets harder to replicate the longer we run.
STD

The attestation standard

Continuous · category ownership

No third-party standard exists for verifying an AI bill. We build toward owning the methodology — the audit format finance teams cite, the "TokenID-verified" mark a provider's invoice can be checked against.

Why it compounds: whoever defines how an AI bill is verified before a standard is written owns the seat at the table — and every audit run is a vote for our methodology becoming the default.
RAD

Pricing-change radar

Continuous · early-warning

The meter changes constantly — a rate cut, a new token type, a caching mode. We monitor every provider's pricing in real time and the moment something shifts, every affected customer is auto-flagged and re-audited against the new card.

Why it stands out: only an independent layer watching all providers at once can do this — a single-customer tool never sees the pattern, and a provider won't warn you its price just disadvantaged you.

Standard-tier recovery is small (0.5–2%) Stated honestly and priced accordingly — standard tier is a "health check" wedge; the large dollars live in enterprise SLA recovery (M8) where success-fee pricing aligns to recovered dollars.
Chat usage has no data path Kept out of committed scope as research, gated on a proven data path (M13). BYOK is the credible lever; seat right-sizing is the data-free fallback. We don't promise coverage we can't deliver.
Enforcement requires sitting in the request path Proxy is opt-in, one env var, ~20–80ms, enabled only after trust is established via observe-first SDK/journal. The free→paid bridge is designed around exactly this adoption curve (M3).
